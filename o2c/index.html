Executive Summary

O2C has built a serious product and given the buyer almost nothing to check it against. The range runs from €175.95 to €449.95, every shoe is offered in Standard and Wide across sizes 36 to 48 with four half sizes, and BikeRadar reports that each size and width gets its own carbon mould. The founder is Steven Nemeth, formerly CEO or director of Bont (the two sources disagree on the title), credited with developing heat-mouldable carbon soles and anatomically shaped toe boxes. The brand launched on 16 January 2026 and started delivering in March. Eight months on there is one independent review, no customer reviews on the site, no Trustpilot profile, no named athletes, no YouTube channel, and a 5.0 star rating displayed on homepage product cards with nothing behind it. At €450, bought online from a Hong Kong registered company, proof is not a nice-to-have. It is the whole job.

The search data shows exactly what the proof vacuum costs. DataForSEO finds o2c-cycling.com ranking for one keyword in Google UK, with no number ones and no measurable organic visits. In Germany, the largest cycling market in Europe, the domain has no data at all. It has 27 referring domains, and a read of them finds one genuine cycling site, the German road-cycling forum rennrad-news.de. The rest are the familiar scraper and spam network plus a myshopify.com preview domain. So one earned link in eight months. Across 14 Google UK category searches O2C appeared in no top-ten result and was cited in no AI Overview. The comparison that matters is not Specialized. It is Quoc, a direct-to-consumer brand of roughly similar scale, which holds 88 UK keywords, 38 UK number ones, an estimated 3,100 UK visits a month and 345 referring domains. Lake holds 97 UK number ones and 1,200 referring domains. This is achievable territory, and O2C holds none of it.

The commercial opening is sitting in plain sight and nobody at O2C is marketing it. The wide-fit cluster in Google UK carries roughly 1,190 searches a month ("wide fit cycling shoes" 590, "wide cycling shoes" 320, "cycling shoes for wide feet" 210, "numb feet cycling" 70) and every one of those terms has a keyword difficulty of 0. Lake ranks first for both "wide cycling shoes" and "cycling shoes for wide feet". Cycling Weekly's 2026 buyer's guide names only Sidi, Bont, Shimano and Lake as brands offering wide fits, and road.cc notes that Sidi's Mega fit is limited to a handful of models. O2C offers wide across 100 per cent of the range, which puts it at parity with Lake and Bont and ahead of nearly everyone else, and the claim appears nowhere in the site's navigation, nowhere as a landing page, and nowhere in the editorial. The brand's most defensible asset is also its least marketed.

Closer to the till, three small contradictions are doing real damage. Product pages advertise "Free returns within 30 days". The refund policy says O2C will send a return shipping label. The FAQs say the customer pays return shipping. No return address is published anywhere, so a European buyer cannot know before purchase whether a return means posting a €450 parcel to the Netherlands or to Hong Kong. The homepage promises free worldwide shipping while the FAQs publish minimum order values for it (over $180 in the USA and Canada, over €160 in Europe, over £150 in the UK, over AUD$260 in Australia). None of this is expensive to fix and all of it is read by exactly the sceptical buyer O2C is asking to spend the most.

The site itself is better built than the search results suggest. DataForSEO's On-Page crawl returns a score of 89.77, with sitemap, robots.txt, SSL, HTTP/2 and the HTTPS redirect all passing. Product pages run 2,400 to 2,530 words with keyword-led titles such as "Vector RG2 Gravel Cycling Shoe, Toray 3K Carbon", the FAQ runs 4,260 words, and there are a Technology page, a Brand Size Comparison tool and a Become a Retailer page. The drag is elsewhere: the homepage H1 is the string "o2c-cycling", the Shopify store handle; 23 pages have no H1 at all, including the collection pages, About, Technology and the FAQ; 58 of 60 pages carry images with no alt text, at 17 missing per product page; and the Korean path /ko-kr/ reproduces the entire English site, producing 49 duplicate pages, 39 duplicate titles and 41 duplicate descriptions. The pages are built. They are not ranking because the brand has no authority and no proof, not because the content is thin.

The three priorities that matter most

1. Put Steven Nemeth's name on the brand and attribute the medals claim to Bont. The About page talks about "the founder" and "17+ years" and never names him. It also states that "those shoes have contributed to 36% of Olympic track cycling medals across the last five Games" without saying whose shoes. BikeRadar attributes the same 36 per cent figure to Bont, so the attribution is already public. Written properly, "shoes I designed at Bont contributed to 36 per cent of Olympic track cycling medals across five Games" is both more honest and considerably more persuasive than the floating version now on the site. Four outlets covered the launch because of who Nemeth is. The website is the only place in the story where he does not appear.

2. Build the proof stack before spending anything on reach. Read the Cyclist review (it is blocked to automated fetch and is currently the entire public verdict on the brand), then seed review units to road.cc, Cycling Weekly, Cyclingnews, Escape Collective and DC Rainmaker for the RT2, and to three to five YouTube fit specialists. Turn on on-site review collection and pull the unbacked 5.0 stars until there are real reviews behind them. Turn the Athlete Programme from an inbound form with no named riders into a published roster. For a brand founded on Olympic and Grand Tour medals, having no identifiable feet in the shoes is the starkest gap in this audit.

3. Make wide fit the brand's territory rather than a specification line. A navigation entry, a dedicated landing page, and symptom-led content on hot spots, numb toes, bunions, toe splay and how to measure a foot in millimetres. The per-size-and-width carbon mould claim is the headline proof and it is currently buried on the Technology page. There are 1,190 UK searches a month at difficulty 0 waiting, Lake is the only brand seriously answering them, and O2C has a better width offer than the guides currently credit it with because none of the guides has heard of it.

i
Overall assessment: a strong product and a strong founder, sold by a brand that is hiding both. Nothing in this audit calls for a repositioning. The proposition (anatomy over aesthetics, biomechanics over fashion) is clear, differentiated and correctly aimed. What is missing is evidence, and the order of work matters more than the volume of it. Proof first, because at €450 nothing else converts. Then discoverability, because the wide-fit demand is uncontested and cheap. Media spend last, because spending to send traffic to a page with no reviews on it wastes the money twice.

Brand Overview

Founding and People

O2C, short for "Obsess to Compete", was announced on 16 January 2026 in a BikeRadar exclusive by Warren Rossiter, followed by BikeBiz on 19 January, Velora Cycling on 22 January and endurance.biz. First deliveries and retail availability were in March 2026. At the date of this audit the brand is roughly eight months old. The founder is Steven Nemeth. BikeRadar's headline calls him a former Bont director; Velora Cycling calls him a former Bont CEO. The exact title, the dates of his tenure and his departure date could not be verified in any source. What is consistent across the coverage is the substance: Velora credits him with having "spearheaded development of heat-mouldable carbon soles and anatomically shaped toe boxes" at Bont, and BikeRadar describes him as having helped develop and grow the company from the original 2007 shoe design by Inze and Alexander Bont. O2C's own About page claims "17+ years" in performance footwear design and BikeBiz reports "nearly two decades".

The corporate entity is O2C Cycling Limited, Hong Kong, business registration 79199066, San Po Kong, with contact addresses info@o2c-cycling.com and warranty@o2c-cycling.com in the footer and on the warranty page. No European or UK entity, office or returns address is disclosed anywhere on the site. One journal byline, "Jonathan Davies", appears on the philosophy post; the role behind that name is unverified. Manufacturing location is not stated on the site, not covered in the FAQs, and BikeRadar also reports it as undisclosed.

!
The founder is the brand's largest marketing asset and he is absent from his own website. Four trade and consumer outlets ran the launch, and every one of them led with his name. A reader arriving at o2c-cycling.com/pages/about finds "the founder" and "17+ years of experience", no name, no photograph, no Bont. A buyer comparing a €450 unknown against a Lake or a Specialized is asking one question first, which is who made this and why should I believe them. The answer exists and is genuinely good. It is simply not on the page.

The Medals Claim

The About page states that "those shoes have contributed to 36% of Olympic track cycling medals across the last five Games, alongside victories at Olympic Time Trial, UCI World Championships, Tour de France, Giro d'Italia, La Vuelta, Paris-Roubaix, MTB and BMX World Cups." BikeRadar independently attributes the same 36 per cent figure to Bont shoes. On O2C's own site the claim floats without attribution, so a reader cannot tell whose shoes are being described. The ambiguity works against the brand in both directions. A sceptical reader assumes the claim belongs to someone else and discounts it. A credulous reader assumes it belongs to O2C, which is a shoe brand eight months old, and the claim collapses the moment they check. Attributing it properly is safer legally, safer reputationally, and considerably more persuasive than the current wording.

Positioning and Tone

The proposition is stated on the homepage as "Real performance starts at the foot. Obsess to Compete defines our approach to fit, biomechanics, and efficiency-because marginal gains aren't optional." The About page adds "We exist to do things differently, not for the sake of it, but because it matters" and "Every product is built anatomically-correct, shaped to reflect the human body in motion." Nemeth's launch quote, used by all three trade outlets, is: "At O2C, everything starts with the foot, not fashion, not trends. I've spent nearly two decades obsessing over how the foot actually works under load."

The register is engineering-absolutist and anti-fashion. The philosophy post states that the brand "is not for everyone" and talks about "questioning industry norms that have gone unchallenged". This reads closer to a materials-science paper than to a lifestyle cycling brand, which is a deliberate and defensible choice in a category where most competitors sell colourways and claimed watts. It also sets a bar. A brand that positions itself on rigour has to be rigorous everywhere a buyer looks, and three contradictory returns policies, an unattributed medals statistic and an unbacked 5.0 star rating all cut against the thing the brand says it is.

Brand Expression

The visual system is white, black and blue #0032A0, set in Geist, with uppercase labels and square corners. It is coherent, premium-appropriate and disciplined. It reads like a components brand rather than a footwear brand, which suits the biomechanics positioning. The weakness in expression is not design, it is population: no founder photograph, no team, no factory, no athletes, no riders. A brand built on anatomy and human measurement currently shows no humans.

Scale Today

Fourteen line items are live on o2c-cycling.com/collections/all: seven shoe SKUs, two sock SKUs, four spare parts and one free sizing tool. Instagram (@o2c.cycling) shows 5,028 followers and 40 posts on Socialblade at 13 September 2026. Nine editorial articles have been published across three separate blogs. The first trade-show appearance is Sea Otter Europe in Girona, 18 to 20 September 2026, booth P2-H, announced in a blog post on 10 September. These numbers set a baseline for the audit rather than a judgement on it. They are the numbers of a launch.

Market and Category Context

Market Sizing

No independent, non-vendor figure for the size of the cycling shoe market was found within the research window, and putting an unverified one into a client document would be worse than leaving the space empty. A syndicated press release states that the bicycle shoe market will reach USD 3.92 billion by 2036, driven by cycling participation and premium footwear demand. The full release returned HTTP 403 on fetch, so only the headline figure from the search result is captured here and the methodology behind it is unverified. The Wide-Fit Gap

This is the most defensible commercial opening in the category and the evidence for it is unusually clean. Cycling Weekly's 2026 buyer's guide names four brands as providing wide-fit options: Sidi, Bont, Shimano and Lake. road.cc's guide to the Sidi range is blunter still, noting that Sidi shoes "are usually made on a fairly narrow last" and that buyers who need them wider should look for the Mega fit, which in the range road.cc surveyed was explicitly offered only on the Genius 7. Lake's own product feed confirms width as a first-class option on essentially every shoe. Specialized retails wide versions of the S-Works Ares 2 through at least one listing. So the genuinely wide-capable field is Lake, Bont, Shimano, Specialized in part and Sidi in part.

O2C offers Standard and Wide on every shoe in the range, in sizes 36 to 48 including four half sizes, with individual carbon moulds per size and width according to BikeRadar. That last point is the hardest thing in the audit to copy and the easiest thing to verify, and it is currently a bullet on a technology page.

The demand is uncontested and free. "Wide fit cycling shoes" draws 590 UK searches a month, "wide cycling shoes" 320, "cycling shoes for wide feet" 210 and "numb feet cycling" 70, and DataForSEO scores every one of those terms at keyword difficulty 0. Lake holds first place for two of them. Nobody is fighting for this, the intent is transactional, and O2C has a product answer that matches or beats the brand currently winning.

Category Structure and Channel

The direct-to-consumer playbook in performance cycling footwear is established rather than novel. Quoc sells direct with a store locator and a 1% For The Planet membership at £135 to £340. Suplest sells direct and through retail with a Cancellara signature edition. Bont sells direct plus distributors. Lake sells direct plus dealers and runs regional Instagram accounts for North America, the Netherlands, France, Canada, South Africa and Colombia. O2C sells direct plus "selected retail partners" according to BikeBiz, and has a B2B login and a Become a Retailer page live, though no dealer list was found on the site. Nothing in O2C's channel design is structurally different from the category. The differentiation has to come from product truth and proof, not from route to market.

The Segment O2C Can Win Without a Brand

The fit-problem buyer is the one segment where O2C can win on product truth rather than on reputation, and it is the segment the search data confirms exists. This buyer does not search for a brand. They search for the symptom or the shape: "cycling shoes for wide feet" (210 a month), "wide fit cycling shoes" (590), "wide cycling shoes" (320), "numb feet cycling" (70), "hot foot cycling" (20, and the only one with a difficulty above 0, at 43), "cycling shoe sizing" (70), "cycling shoe fit" (40 with a cost per click of £2.37). They are already in pain, they have already spent money on the wrong answer, and a brand that explains the problem better than anyone else wins the sale before it wins the brand preference. That is the exact opposite of the current site, which explains the philosophy well and the symptoms not at all.

The post-bike-fit upgrader deserves separate mention because the acquisition cost is close to zero and the infrastructure already exists. A rider walking out of a fitting studio having been told their shoes are the constraint is the highest-intent buyer in this category. Fitters are the referrer. O2C has a B2B login and a Become a Retailer page and no visible fitter programme, no fitter-facing content and no fitter listing. The backlink data reinforces the point: Lake earns links from bike fitters and retailers such as bikefit.lu and torqfitness.co.uk. That channel is both a sales channel and a link channel at the same time.

Messaging and Site Experience Review

The First Five Seconds

A first-time visitor learns that these are premium cycling shoes for road, gravel and triathlon, made of carbon, under the line "Obsess to Compete", with free worldwide shipping and duties included. That is a clear and adequate five seconds. What the visitor does not learn is who makes these shoes, why they should believe the claims, or that the founder designed the shoes credited with a third of recent Olympic track medals.

Proposition

Strong and coherent. Anatomy over aesthetics, biomechanics over fashion, fit as the basis of efficiency. It is genuinely differentiated in a category where most competitors sell colourways and claimed stiffness numbers. The proposition is not the problem anywhere in this audit.

Proof

Thin to absent on the pages where it decides a sale. The homepage shows 5.0-star ratings on featured product cards. The AXIS RS1, AXIS RK1 and VECTOR RG2 product pages show no customer reviews at all. A perfect rating with nothing behind it reads as decoration, and it erodes trust with precisely the sceptical, detail-reading buyer the brand says it is built for. There is no Trustpilot profile, no on-page reviews, no founder name, no founder photograph, no team, no factory disclosure, no athletes and no retailer list.

!
Pull the 5.0 stars this week. A rating with no reviews behind it is the one element on the site that actively costs trust rather than merely failing to build it. Replace it with review collection (a post-delivery email at 30 days, when the buyer has ridden the shoes) and put the stars back when there are real reviews under them. Ten honest reviews averaging 4.6 will sell more €450 shoes than a decorative 5.0.

Trust Signals That Are Working

It is worth being precise about what is good here, because there is more of it than the gaps suggest. Duties and taxes included with "no additional charges upon delivery" removes the biggest single objection to a cross-border premium purchase and very few competitors state it as plainly. The 24-month EU warranty exceeds the 12-month standard. The lifetime dial warranty is unusual in the category and is a real answer to the most common failure mode in a dial-closure shoe. Those signals are individually strong and collectively undermined by sitting next to three contradictory returns statements and an unearned star rating. Trust is read as a whole. One visible inconsistency puts every other claim on the page under suspicion, which is the specific reason the returns fix is the cheapest high-value action in this audit.

Tone of Voice

The writing is disciplined, technical and confident, and it matches the visual identity. Two adjustments would help. First, the register needs a second gear: the Technology page speaks to an engineer, and the buyer with burning feet on hour four needs the same information in their own words. Second, the brand needs a first person. "We exist to do things differently" is a corporate voice for a company that is, as far as any public record shows, one person with 17 years of relevant work behind him. Nemeth speaking as himself, as he does in the press quotes, is more convincing than the site's institutional voice, and costs nothing to adopt.

The Brand Name Problem

"o2c" draws 170 UK searches a month at a cost per click of £108.54, because the initials belong to order-to-cash finance software. "o2c cycling" falls below DataForSEO's reporting threshold, meaning branded demand for the shoe brand is not yet measurable at all. This is not fatal and it is worth knowing. It means two things in practice: every mention of the brand in press, on packaging and in social bios should use a qualified form ("O2C Cycling") so that the searches people do actually find the shoes, and paid search on the bare initials would be expensive and largely wasted.

SWOT Summary

Strengths

  • A founder with verifiable elite pedigree: former Bont CEO or director, credited with heat-mouldable carbon soles and anatomic toe boxes (BikeRadar, Velora Cycling).
  • Four trade and consumer outlets covered the launch, including a BikeRadar exclusive with full spec tables.
  • Wide fit on 100 per cent of the range, with individual carbon moulds per size and width, a claim only Lake and Bont can match and none states as precisely.
  • Concrete, checkable technical differentiators: 3.2mm road stack, 4.0mm gravel stack, Toray T800 UD layups, sizes 36 to 48 with four half sizes.
  • Lifetime dial warranty plus a full spare-parts programme (dials, TPU protectors, insoles, RG2 sole pads).
  • Best sizing toolkit in the comparison set: brand-comparison product, millimetre-based guidance, AI assistant.
  • Instagram engagement at 0.69 per cent, ahead of fizik, Specialized, Bont and Lake despite a fraction of their audience.
  • Duties and taxes included, which removes the biggest cross-border purchase objection.
  • Product pages of 2,400 to 2,530 words with keyword-led titles and a 4,260-word FAQ, well above category norms.
  • A disciplined, coherent brand identity that matches the engineering positioning.

Weaknesses

  • One independent review in eight months, and it could not be read for this audit. Zero buyer's-guide inclusions.
  • Zero on-site customer reviews, with 5.0-star ratings displayed on the homepage regardless.
  • The founder is anonymous on his own website and the Bont lineage is never claimed.
  • The 36 per cent Olympic medals claim is unattributed, so it reads as ambiguous rather than impressive.
  • Three contradictory returns messages across three pages, and no published return address.
  • "Free worldwide shipping" on the homepage against published minimum order thresholds in the FAQs.
  • No named athletes or teams; the Athlete Programme is an inbound form with no roster and no benefits.
  • Nine articles in eight months, three of them product copy, split across three blogs, with the footer "Journal" link returning a 404.
  • One UK keyword, no UK number ones, no German presence, 27 referring domains with one genuine cycling link.
  • Homepage H1 of "o2c-cycling", 23 pages with no H1, 58 of 60 pages with missing image alt text, 49 duplicate pages from /ko-kr/.
  • No YouTube, no TikTok, no Strava club, no Trustpilot profile.
  • Manufacturing location undisclosed on the site and in all press coverage.
  • Weights quoted at size 42 on site against size 45 in press, which are not comparable.
  • Two or three colourways across the whole range, with the RK1 and RG2 single-colour.

Opportunities

  • Own the wide-fit and foot-problem entry points: roughly 1,190 UK searches a month at difficulty 0, with only Lake seriously answering them.
  • Put Nemeth's name and face on everything. The press already leads with it and the website does not.
  • Seed review units to road.cc, Cyclingnews, Cycling Weekly, Escape Collective, DC Rainmaker and the YouTube fit community. One wide-fit-specific review would be worth more than a month of media spend.
  • The bike-fitter channel, which is both the highest-intent referrer for a €450 anatomic shoe and, on the evidence of Lake's backlink profile, a link source.
  • Comparison content against competitor brand terms, a pool of over 8,000 UK searches a month at difficulty 0 to 2, using the free Brand Size Comparison tool as the asset.
  • Sea Otter Europe (18 to 20 September 2026, Girona, booth P2-H) as a content, PR and retailer-recruitment moment, currently supported by one blog post.
  • Germany, where the brand's only genuine earned link already comes from and where it currently registers nothing.
  • Repairability and the lifetime dial warranty as a value and sustainability story that nobody in the category is telling well.
  • Korea storefronts already exist, which makes Korea a defined market rather than an accident of the theme.

Threats

  • Lake and Bont have seven to eleven times the Instagram audience and decades of wide-fit authority with the buyer who matters most to O2C.
  • Specialized at $600 and Lake at $599.99 define the ceiling, and O2C at €449.95 sits in the most crowded band (Lake CX400 $449.99, Quoc M3 Pro £340, fizik £334.99, Sidi Sixty £330).
  • The category buys on tested verdicts. A review vacuum at this price is a commercial risk to the business, not a marketing inconvenience.
  • Hong Kong registration, undisclosed manufacturing and no EU returns address compound into a single trust problem for European buyers.
  • If the one Cyclist review is unfavourable, it is currently the entire public verdict on the brand and it will be the first thing a buyer finds.
  • Publishers own the category SERPs (Cycling Weekly in 12 of 14, Cyclist in 7, BikeRadar in 6), so visibility depends on editorial decisions O2C does not control.
  • The initials collide with order-to-cash finance software, which makes unqualified brand search expensive and unhelpful.

Strategic Implications

Six priorities follow from the evidence. None asks O2C to change what it is or what it sells. Two of them cost almost nothing and should be done this week. The one that decides the next two years takes two quarters, which is why it has to start now rather than after the cheap fixes are finished.

Priority 1 · Critical · Impact: high · Effort: low · This week

Name Steven Nemeth and attribute the medals claim to Bont

Rewrite the About page around the person. Name him, photograph him, state the Bont role in the words the press already used, and say what he did there: heat-mouldable carbon soles and anatomically shaped toe boxes. Put a one-line version on the homepage, on every product page near the price, in the Instagram bio, and on the packaging insert. "Designed by the man who led Bont" is a proposition. "17+ years of experience" is a filler phrase that any brand could write.

At the same time, fix the medals sentence. The current wording, "those shoes have contributed to 36% of Olympic track cycling medals across the last five Games", does not say whose shoes and therefore persuades nobody and protects nobody. BikeRadar has already attributed the 36 per cent figure to Bont in public, so the safe version is also the strong version: shoes Nemeth designed at Bont contributed to 36 per cent of Olympic track cycling medals across five Games. The same rewrite should settle the title question internally, since BikeRadar says director and Velora says CEO, and neither dates his tenure. Use the title he can evidence.

Publish the manufacturing location in the same pass. In a category where "handmade in Italy" and "made in Australia" are load-bearing claims, silence is read as the least flattering possible answer, and the FAQ currently says nothing at all on the subject.

Priority 2 · Critical · Impact: high · Effort: low · This week

Give one answer on returns, shipping and ratings

Decide who pays for a return, write it once, and publish the same sentence on product pages, in the refund policy and in the FAQs. Publish a return address, or at minimum the destination country, before the buyer reaches checkout. A European spending €450 needs to know whether a return means a parcel to the Netherlands or to Hong Kong, and at present the site does not tell them at any point in the journey.

Reconcile the shipping claim in the same edit. Either free worldwide shipping is unconditional, in which case remove the minimum order values from the FAQs, or it has thresholds, in which case say so on the homepage. Move the delivery window (5 to 7 business days processing plus 2 to 14 days in transit) out of the FAQs and onto the product page, because a buyer who discovers a three-week worst case after ordering is a support ticket and a refund, while a buyer who knows in advance is a buyer.

Remove the 5.0-star ratings from the homepage product cards until real reviews sit behind them. This is the single element on the site that costs trust rather than merely failing to earn it, and it is inconsistent with a brand whose whole argument is rigour.

Priority 3 · Critical · Impact: high · Effort: high · This quarter and next

Build the proof stack before spending anything on reach

Start by reading the Cyclist review of the Axis RK1 by hand. It is blocked to automated fetch, it is the only independent verdict in existence, and every decision below depends on what it says. If it is good, it is the centrepiece of the next six months of marketing. If it is not, the product feedback in it is worth more than the coverage would have been.

Then seed review units deliberately rather than hopefully: road.cc, Cycling Weekly, Cyclingnews and Escape Collective for the Axis models, DC Rainmaker and 220 Triathlon for the RT2, and three to five YouTube specialists who cover fit rather than kit. Pitch the wide-fit angle specifically, because a wide-fit review is a review nobody else in the set can be given. Ask for buyer's-guide inclusion explicitly, since Cycling Weekly's guide appears in 12 of the 14 category SERPs measured and O2C is in none of them.

Turn on on-site review collection with a post-delivery request at around 30 days, when the buyer has actually ridden in the shoes. Open a Trustpilot profile and ask for reviews there too, because a buyer checking an unfamiliar Hong Kong registered company will look for one and currently finds nothing. Then turn the Athlete Programme from a form into a roster: name the riders, publish what they ride and where, and show feet in shoes. A handful of named elite amateurs and one credible professional would close the widest gap in this audit.

Priority 4 · High · Impact: high · Effort: medium · This quarter

Make wide fit the brand's territory, not a specification line

Add a wide-fit entry to the main navigation, build a landing page behind it, and make the per-size-and-width carbon mould the headline proof on that page rather than a bullet on /pages/technology. Explain it the way a buyer would check it: a size 42 wide is not a size 42 standard stretched sideways, it is a different mould, and here is what that changes under load. Show the moulds. This is the most checkable and least copyable claim the brand owns.

Behind the landing page, write the symptom content that does not currently exist anywhere on the site: hot spots and why they happen, numb toes and neuroma, bunions, toe splay, how to measure a foot in millimetres, how stack height interacts with saddle height, and what to do when a shoe fits in the shop and hurts at hour four. The demand is measurable and uncontested ("wide fit cycling shoes" 590 a month, "wide cycling shoes" 320, "cycling shoes for wide feet" 210, "numb feet cycling" 70, all at difficulty 0), and Lake currently takes two of those first places with product pages rather than with better answers.

Write the comparison content in the same programme, because it targets the largest pool of cheap demand in the category: "lake cycling shoes" 2,400 a month, "fizik cycling shoes" 1,300, "shimano cycling shoes" 1,300, "sidi cycling shoes" 1,000, "specialized cycling shoes" 880, "quoc shoes" 880, "bont cycling shoes" 720, almost all at difficulty 0. The free Brand Size Comparison product is already the right asset for these pages. It simply needs a page per brand, honestly written, with the sizing table rendered as server-side HTML so that a search engine can read it.

Priority 5 · High · Impact: medium · Effort: low · This month

Clear the technical and structural drag

Replace the homepage H1, currently the Shopify store handle "o2c-cycling", with a descriptive line that names the category. Add H1s to the 23 pages without one, starting with the collection pages, About, Technology and the FAQ. Add alt text across the 58 pages carrying images without it, at 17 missing per product page, which also makes the product photography visible to Google Images and Google Shopping. Write meta descriptions for the nine policy pages and lengthen the 15 titles flagged as too short.

Resolve the Korean duplication. The /ko-kr/ path reproduces the entire English site including English titles and descriptions, which is the cause of 49 duplicate pages, 39 duplicate titles and 41 duplicate descriptions. Either translate it properly and declare hreflang across /ko-kr/, /en-kr and the main site, or remove it from the index. Confirm the canonicals on the 58 pages carrying them point where they should, and settle the www against non-www question that DataForSEO's canonicalisation test flagged.

Consolidate the three blogs into one Journal with redirects from /blogs/stories, /blogs/discover and /blogs/shoes, and fix the footer link that currently 404s at /blogs/journal. Verify in a browser whether the Find Your Size and Sizing Chart tables render server-side; if they do not, mirror them as HTML, because a sizing table that only exists in JavaScript is invisible to search engines and AI assistants as well as to the crawler. Finally, address the 3.0-second homepage waiting time and the render-blocking resources on 58 pages.

Priority 6 · Medium · Impact: medium · Effort: medium · From Sea Otter Europe onwards

Earn links the way this category actually earns them

The backlink profiles of the competitor set show that links in cycling footwear come from races, teams, fitters and stockists, not from blogging. DMT is linked from strade-bianche.it, milanosanremo.it, ilombardia.it, maratona.it and its pro team. fizik from Movistar, UAE Team ADQ, Crank Brothers and Brooks England. Quoc from road.cc and European retailers. Lake from bike fitters such as bikefit.lu and torqfitness.co.uk. Sidi from eroica.cc and giroe.it. Twenty-seven referring domains is not a content problem, it is a relationships problem.

Sea Otter Europe in Girona on 18 to 20 September 2026 is the first opportunity and it arrives with one blog post behind it. Treat it as a retailer and fitter recruitment exercise: a target list of European stockists and fit studios, a trade offer, a dealer locator page to list them on afterwards, and a fitter programme with sample sizes and a referral route. Every fitter who lists O2C is a link, a stockist and a source of the highest-intent buyer in the category at the same time.

Germany deserves a named place in this plan. It is the largest cycling market in Europe, the brand registers no keywords there at all, and its single genuine earned link comes from rennrad-news.de. German product pages, a German retailer push and a presence in German forums would build on interest that already exists rather than manufacturing it. That is a better use of localisation effort than the current Korean storefront, which duplicates the English site without translating it.

Data sources: o2c-cycling.com and its sitemaps, policy, product and editorial pages; competitor websites and published price lists including lakecycling.com, quoc.cc and suplest.com product feeds; Socialblade for Instagram figures; trade and consumer cycling press (BikeRadar, BikeBiz, Velora Cycling, endurance.biz, Cycling Weekly, road.cc, Bikerumor, Cyclist); and DataForSEO (Labs domain rank overview and keyword overview, SERP API with Google AI Overviews, Backlinks summary, On-Page crawl) for Google UK, Germany and the United States, run on 13 September 2026 through the Manual Focus marketing-audit skill. Traffic and visit figures from DataForSEO are modelled estimates. No SimilarWeb data was used. The Meta Ad Library, Instagram profile pages and the Cyclist review were blocked to automated access, and those gaps are stated in the sections they affect. Observations about site content, pricing and policies are a point-in-time snapshot taken eight months after launch. No first-party analytics, sales or order data were used.
