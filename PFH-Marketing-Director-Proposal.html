01 · Executive summary

You've already done the hard part. Here's where I'd focus.

Precision Fuel & Hydration is an independent, athlete-founded brand with an advantage rivals cannot easily copy, because the free planner, the sweat test and the expert consultations capture athletes, personalise their fuelling and turn them into loyal customers, and in the last two years you have added the most visible branding asset in the sport on top, the global IRONMAN and 70.3 hydration partnership. 03 · The company

Who Precision Fuel & Hydration is

The brief only makes sense against what you have actually built, so before the findings, here is my understanding of the business, condensed from the research.

Andy Blow founded the business in 2011 after cramp and a bout of hyponatremia wrecked his own racing, and his work with heart surgeon Professor Raj Jutley revealed why, because sodium losses vary enormously between athletes. That insight, that hydration is individual, is still the whole brand. The company rebranded from Precision Hydration to Precision Fuel & Hydration in February 2022 as it expanded from electrolytes into carbohydrate fuelling, and today it is bootstrapped, founder-led and selling into more than 90 countries with operations on three continents.

05 · The audit

What I found, and where I think I can help

With the brief and the context in place, this is what I found when I went through your marketing the way a new customer, a journalist and a competitor would. It all comes from the outside, I have not seen your analytics, CRM or margins, so read each finding as an informed hypothesis offered with respect for what you have already built.

1 Brand & positioning ›

The hydration story is yours and nobody credibly challenges it, and the clarity work on the range is visibly underway, the shop's category tabs give buyers a clean decision layer and renaming the PF 60 Drink Mix to Carb & Electrolyte Drink Mix shows you are already moving the naming toward plain English. The gap now sits between your own pages, because the fuel pages set a standard the hydration pages have not caught up with yet.

Where I would help
  • Finish the job on the hydration pages. The fuel pages lead with their numbers, "60g of carb and 1,000mg of sodium per litre, the simplest way to get a decent amount of fuel and electrolytes in from your bottles," and the PH 1500 page already tells a heavy, salty sweater it is for them. What the page never says is what 1500 means, the sodium per litre logic that is your whole naming system, or how it compares to a typical sports drink, a line that currently sits in the page metadata rather than the copy. And the strength toggle lets a shopper switch between 500, 1000 and 1500 without anything helping them choose, so a small "not sure which strength you are?" link beneath it, pointing to the planner, would close that loop with the tool you already own.
  • A named carb story. Maurten has "hydrogel," one word that carries its science into every review. Your fuel range is described generically, "hydrolysed carbohydrate." Work with the sports science team to name and codify your carb system, the ratio, the gut-training protocol, the PF numbering, so athletes and reviewers have a word for why your fuel is different, the way "start with your sweat" already does that job for hydration.
  • A wider bench of expert voices. Andy's voice built the brand's authority, and the pull he has with press and podcasts proves the expert-led model works. The opportunity is to multiply it by developing Dr Shepherd, Dr Hunt and the athlete-support scientists into named public voices of their own, with bylines, video and press availability, so more of the demand for PF&H expertise gets met and Andy's time is saved for the moments only a founder can carry.
2 Website, funnel & retention ›

Having completed the planner end to end, I can't name a stronger lead magnet in the category, and the execution matches the idea, roughly a dozen light steps, the email ask held back until the plan is earned, opt-in consent done properly, and a personalised taster pack with one-click add to cart waiting at the end, backed by the Fuel Skool email series. The opportunity begins after that first order. The taster pack lands at £37.49 with the £49.99 free-shipping threshold printed just below it, yet nothing suggests the £12.50 top-up that would earn it, no subscription or referral moment appears anywhere in the plan, pack or emails, the "Share your numbers" feature carries no referral mechanic, and the race date you collect is not yet visibly driving countdown or post-race restock flows.

Where I would help
  • Turn the shipping line into a nudge. The taster pack page already states free shipping over £49.99 just under the £37.49 price, so the information is there but the invitation is not. A simple "you are £12.50 from free shipping, athletes usually add a box of gels" prompt, with the add-on one click away, lifts order value with no discounting at all.
  • A subscription moment after the taster pack. Subscribe & Save sits on every product page, but the planner journey, the taster pack and the Fuel Skool emails never mention it. Once an athlete has trained on their taster pack, a timed prompt moves the products their plan proved onto subscription, and because you hold their race date, the timing is predictable rather than guessed.
  • Referral built into "Share your numbers." The share feature already exists with no mechanic attached. Attach a give-get, share your plan, your friend gets a taster-pack offer and you get credit, and the sharing that already happens becomes measurable acquisition.
  • Lifecycle flows driven by the race date. The planner captures each athlete's event date and nothing visibly uses it. Build the obvious flows against it, a taper-week checklist, a race-week restock reminder, a day-after "how did it go" with a review ask and a prompt to plan the next race, so the journey continues after Fuel Skool's five emails end.

The IRONMAN partnership is a landmark, the strongest brand real estate in endurance sport, and it sets up a wonderful next step, turning that reach into named athletes. Where I would help

What difficulty means. Each market gets its own 0 to 100 score, because the same term can be easy in the UK and contested in the US or the reverse. The score estimates how hard it is to break into the organic top 10 for that term, based on the backlink strength of the pages currently ranking there. A low score means the current top 10 is held by pages with little authority behind them, so a dedicated, well-built page on a domain of your strength should rank quickly. A high score means the top 10 is dominated by strongly linked pages and winning it is a longer content-and-links project. It is a link-strength proxy rather than a guarantee, which is why each play is matched to the score.

09 · Retail expansion

Retail as paid-for trial

One channel from the review deserves its own section, because retail is where a DTC brand can most easily buy new trial or most easily lose margin, and the difference between the two is discipline. My governing test for every retail decision is simple. Does it put product in the hands of an athlete you cannot reach on Meta or Google, and does it route them back to the planner?

You are already in retail through Sigma Sports, Healf, The Feed, Amazon and Fuel Goods, so the question is which channels to lean into and how to instrument them so they feed DTC. The reference set makes the choice clear. Maurten runs selective specialty plus owned events as a credibility placement, Science in Sport went mass and ended up commoditised and promotion-driven, and LMNT only entered grocery after six years of DTC scale and a purpose-built everyday SKU. Maurten's discipline is the model here, and SiS's route is the warning.

How retail feeds DTC instead of cannibalising it. A unique QR per retailer in every pack drives to the free planner, so the core KPI becomes planner completions sourced from retail rather than units shifted. Measure trial-to-DTC conversion and an effective retail CAC (margin sacrificed plus trade cost, divided by DTC customers acquired) against blended paid CAC, and cut any channel that misses the hurdle within two to three quarters.
10 · Activation, content & go-to-market

The IRONMAN strategy, and when to repeat it

Everything above only compounds if it runs as one way of working rather than a list of initiatives, and you have already built that way of working once, with IRONMAN. Whether that partnership is hitting its objectives is something only your numbers can answer, and it is one of the first questions I would ask, because the strategy only deserves repeating once it has. This section looks at how you activate and publish today, where the headroom is, and what would make the IRONMAN strategy repeatable for the next category and market.

What is working
  • Race activation is best-in-class trial, with on-course product, expo presence and courses loaded into the planner. Few brands fuse sponsorship and product this well.
  • The content engine has real depth, hundreds of cited, scientist-bylined pieces plus athlete case studies with intake data no competitor publishes.
  • The tone is right, evidence-led but unintimidating, athlete to athlete.
Where there is headroom
  • Activation could capture more of what it earns, because the impressions made on course deserve a QR, code or sweat-test loop to turn them into named athletes.
  • The case-study gold deserves more formats, short-form video, carousels and data graphics, so it reaches the younger audience where they live.
  • A race-week routine would let the evergreen library ride each IRONMAN's attention spike as well.
  • More voices could share the platform, because the scientist bench is a media asset in its own right alongside Andy.

What I would change first

  • Capture-first activation. Every event gets a unique QR and planner preset, sweat-test stations at expo, and a measured sampled-to-repeat cohort, because the sponsorship already paid for the audience and capture is the cheap part.
  • Atomise the science. Each case study and pillar becomes a stack, a 60-second vertical video, a data graphic, a carousel, a newsletter item and a podcast segment, so the same research cost buys five times the surface area.
  • A race-week editorial calendar. For every major race on the partnership calendar, a course-specific fuelling guide, an athlete preview with real numbers, live race-day content and a post-race analysis.
  • Build the bench. Make Shepherd, Hunt and the sports-science team named, followable voices, and the default expert sources offered to press.

The IRONMAN strategy, written down. Once the partnership has hit its objectives, a new category or market runs the same six steps.

13 · The research & measurement stack

Keeping this evidence-led

And to keep that scoreboard honest, the stack behind it, part of which produced this document and the rest of which I would add in-seat so decisions stay on live data rather than opinion.

Search & content

  • DataForSEO (used here) or Ahrefs for rankings, gaps and backlinks, with Google Search Console and a Screaming Frog crawl for the technical layer.
  • AI-visibility tracking (Profound, Otterly or DataForSEO's LLM-mentions data) to measure whether ChatGPT and Perplexity cite you on fuelling questions, which nobody in the category watches yet.

Audience & community

  • SparkToro for where endurance athletes spend attention, and post-purchase surveys (Fairing or KnoCommerce) for real how-did-you-hear attribution.
  • Reddit, Slowtwitch and TrainerRoad forum mining for language, objections and sentiment, free and unfiltered.

Competitor & paid intelligence

  • Meta Ad Library, Google Ads Transparency Center and TikTok Creative Center, all free, for every rival's live creative and offers.
  • HypeAuditor or Modash for creator and follower benchmarking (used here), and SimilarWeb for traffic-mix estimates.

Media, CX & measurement

  • Muck Rack or Meltwater to turn the directional media read into a real clip count and share of voice.
  • Microsoft Clarity or Hotjar for session recordings on the planner funnel, GA4 with strict UTM discipline before any attribution platform, and incrementality holdouts as the honest test.
