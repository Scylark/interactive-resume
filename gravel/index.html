The gravel gap

Five years of data on gravel, road, track and mountain bike. Gravel is the one discipline still growing in a shrinking UK market, and British Cycling has barely started.

11 October 2026Global, UK focus2021 to 2026By Manual Focus

00Scope and data

This report compares the four disciplines most British riders choose between, which are gravel, road, track and mountain bike, over the five years from autumn 2021. It looks at global signals first and then at the UK, and asks one question. Where should British Cycling put its next pound of participation effort?

What the data cannot show. No public source counts gravel riders in the UK. British Cycling does not publish licence or event numbers for every discipline, Strava stopped publishing gravel figures after its 2023 report, and UK retailers do not publish sales by category. Search data stands in for interest, and event entries stand in for participation. Where a figure is modelled, estimated or our own judgement, it is labelled. Google Trends values are relative, so the report compares disciplines with each other rather than quoting them as volumes.

01Executive summary

British cycling is shrinking. Adult leisure and sport cycling in England fell from 7.3 million people in 2019/20 to about 6.1 million, and has stayed there. British Cycling lost 15% of its members in two years, from 137,968 to 117,475. Road racing, the sport's traditional core, is losing races and riders, with circuit races down 14% and road races down 16% since 2022 and 23 road races cancelled in 2025. Inside that decline, one discipline is moving the other way.

Gravel is the growth story of the last five years. The UCI Gravel World Series went from 11 events in 2022 to 47 in 32 countries in 2026. The World Championships grew from 556 starters in 2022 to more than 2,500 entries in 2025, and 345 of those were British, the third largest nation. In the UK, Bicycle Association data shows about 30,000 gravel bikes sold in the year to July 2024, up 284% on pre-Covid, while the wider market fell to its lowest level in around 50 years. In Germany, gravel overtook road bikes outright and reached 10% of non-electric bike sales in 2025. Scotland's Gralloch, the only UK round of the UCI series, grew from 1,230 riders in 2023 to more than 3,000 from 47 nations in 2026. Dirty Reiver sold out its 2026 edition in six hours.

Search data tells the same story from the demand side. Across the four disciplines, gravel's share of UK search interest in racing grew from 8% to 42% over five years. In 2024/25 gravel race searches overtook road race searches in the UK, and in 2025/26 they passed mountain bike race searches too. UK searches for gravel race events rose 49% in three years, while searches for road racing and road cycling events fell by 60% or more. Shopping searches fell for every bike type, which reflects the market, though in Google Trends gravel's share of bike searches still rose from 10% to 12%.

British Cycling has been slow to follow. It did not recognise gravel as a racing category until mid-2025. Its first official National Gravel Championships took place at Ae Forest in August 2026, and after that weekend its calendar listed no other gravel events. Its strategy to 2029 does not mention gravel. Its website ranks for 42 gravel search terms worth an estimated 333 visits a month, against 8,815 for mountain biking and 7,472 for road. For "gravel events uk" and "gravel cycling uk" it does not appear in Google's top ten at all. The riders, the races and the search demand are being served by independent organisers, media and brands.

The argument. This is not a case for taking money from the track. UK Sport's £38.95m for LA28 is tied to Olympic and Paralympic disciplines, and gravel is not one of them. It is a case for pointing British Cycling's own participation, membership and commercial effort at the discipline that is bringing new riders into the sport. Gravel has no traffic and no policing bill, which are the two problems killing road racing, and its riders already pay to race. British Cycling needs a gravel product that gives them a reason to join.

02The discipline

Gravel is drop-bar riding on mixed surfaces, including forest roads, byways, farm tracks and quiet lanes. It sits between road and mountain bike. The bike looks like a road bike with wider tyres and more clearance, and it works as an all-rounder. The racing ranges from short UCI qualifiers to 200-mile endurance events, and most events let amateurs start alongside professionals.

How it grew

  • Grassroots first, governance later. The format came from the US Midwest. Unbound Gravel had 34 starters in 2006 and has run with a field of about 5,000 since 2024. The UCI created its Gravel World Series and World Championships in 2022, 16 years after Unbound began.
  • The industry backed it with product. SRAM launched its XPLR gravel groupset in August 2021 and Shimano launched GRX Di2 1x12 in June 2025. Specialized topped Strava's gravel bike rankings in 2024.
  • Riders voted with their uploads. Strava's 2023 report recorded a 55% rise in athletes logging a gravel ride, against 13% for mountain biking and 23% for e-bikes.
  • The pros arrived. The Life Time Grand Prix purse rose from $250,000 in 2022 to $590,000 for 2026. WorldTour riders now race gravel worlds, and in the US the number of UCI-level road teams fell from 19 to 11 over a decade while gravel grew.
!

Not every signal points up. Unbound has plateaued at about 5,000 riders. SBT GRVL was capped at 1,800 by its county permit for 2025. The Gravel Earth series cut its calendar from 25 events to 12 for 2026. The 2026 World Championships in Australia drew only three WorldTour men. Gravel is maturing and some events are finding their ceiling. That makes the next point more important. The growth is moving from the US to Europe and the UK, which is exactly where British Cycling operates.

The pattern is the same in every market with published category data. The total market shrinks after the pandemic boom, road and mountain bike lose share, and gravel holds or grows. A UK shop quoted by Cycling Weekly put it plainly, with gravel sales up 110% in a year and road down 45%. One shop is an anecdote, but it matches the national figures.

PESTLE

FactorWhat it means for gravel and British Cycling
PoliticalRoad racing needs police support. One organiser was quoted £35,000 for 30 police motorbikes in 2024, against £3,500 for six in 2019, and cancelled. Gravel runs on private estates and forestry land with no traffic management.
EconomicCost-of-living pressure is the reason British Cycling gave for falling membership. Flagship gravel events still sell out within hours, which suggests riders will pay for the right product.
SocialGravel offers hard riding away from cars, and it draws first-timers. More than 40% of riders at the first Gralloch were in their first gravel race.
TechnologicalRoute platforms such as Komoot and Strava make off-road routes easy to find and share, and they now rank above governing bodies for "gravel cycling uk" in Google.
LegalRights of way are the hard constraint. Bridleways and byways in England and Wales limit course design, Scotland's access rights are more open, and Ae Forest, The Gralloch and Dirty Reiver all sit in Scotland or on the border. Until British Cycling recognised gravel, insurance options for organisers were limited.
EnvironmentalEvents on forestry land need landowner agreements. Grinduro took a year off over Forestry Commission charges before moving to a private estate in 2025. Land access is a partnership job British Cycling is well placed to do.

04The rider

No one publishes a demographic profile of UK gravel riders, so this section uses event and federation data. It should be tested against British Cycling's own member data and event entries.

  • New to racing. More than 40% of the 1,230 riders at the first Gralloch in 2023 had never raced gravel before. That is new money and new members for any governing body that can capture them.
  • More women than road racing. Women were 25% of Unbound's 2025 field. British road racing had 704 women among just under 4,000 racers in 2025, about 18%. SBT GRVL's 2020 edition sold out in under 25 minutes with 75% more women than the year before.
  • Already competing for Britain. 345 British riders entered the 2025 Gravel World Championships in the Netherlands, more than from Germany, France or the USA. They qualified through UCI series events, and the only one in the UK is The Gralloch.
  • Travelling. The Gralloch drew 47 nations in 2026, The Traka expected riders of 74 nationalities, and Unbound's 2025 field brought an estimated $21.8m of economic impact to one Kansas county. Gravel riders travel for events, which matters for regional funding and tourism partners.
i

Same riders, different door. The gravel rider is often a road rider who stopped racing on roads, a mountain biker who wants distance, or a new adult rider who wants to avoid traffic. They are inside cycling's audience and outside British Cycling's products.

06British Cycling's discipline portfolio

A Boston matrix usually maps a company's products. Here it maps British Cycling's disciplines by market growth (participation and interest) and by British Cycling's share of that activity (how much of it runs through BC events, licences and membership). The placements are our judgement from the evidence above.

Stars

None today. Gravel should be here within one strategy cycle if British Cycling builds the product.

Question mark

Gravel. The fastest-growing discipline in every dataset available, with almost none of it running through British Cycling. One national championship, no series, no gravel membership product, 42 ranking keywords.

Cash cows

Track. Most of Britain's 11 Paris medals came on the track, and it anchors the UK Sport funding. Low growth, but it pays for itself in profile.

Mountain bike XC. Olympic gold in Paris through Tom Pidcock, a strong elite story and the largest BC search footprint.

Dogs

Domestic road racing. High British Cycling share, falling numbers of races and racers, and rising policing costs. Still important, but it cannot carry growth on its own.

Elite road is outside this matrix. The Tour of Britain and the national championships are commercial assets for British Cycling and the Lloyds partnership, separate from grassroots road racing.

The UK's gravel calendar was built by independent organisers, mostly in Scotland and the north, without British Cycling. That is a strength, because the events are good and riders trust them. It is also British Cycling's opportunity, because those organisers face the same problems road organisers do, which are insurance, land access, rising costs and marketing, and they need a governing body that helps rather than charges.

!

The road warning. Road racing did not decline because riders lost interest in racing. It declined because the cost and difficulty of putting on a race rose faster than the support for organisers. Circuit races fell from about 700 to 600 between 2022 and 2025. British Cycling has the chance to avoid repeating that with gravel by building organiser support in from the start.

British Cycling's best gravel results come from a handful of guide pages and the championship page. Those rank and earn AI citations, which shows the domain's authority works when the content exists. 11SWOT, gravel for British Cycling

Strengths

  • A trusted national brand with Lloyds as lead partner, already backing a gravel championship
  • Domain authority that already earns AI citations for gravel guides
  • A club network and coaching structure no independent organiser has
  • British riders already competing in numbers, with 345 at the 2025 Worlds

Weaknesses

  • Gravel recognised only from mid-2025, one event on the calendar
  • No mention of gravel in the strategy to 2029
  • 42 gravel ranking keywords against 300+ for every other discipline
  • Membership down 15% in two years, with no product built for non-racing riders who want events

Opportunities

  • A national gravel series feeding UCI qualification through The Gralloch
  • An organiser package covering insurance, land access and marketing
  • A gravel membership tier for riders who pay to race but have no reason to join
  • More women and more first-time racers than road racing attracts

Threats

  • Independent organisers and brands set the standard and see no need for British Cycling
  • Rights-of-way limits in England and Wales cap growth south of the border
  • Event fatigue and plateaus, as seen with Unbound and Gravel Earth
  • Sanctioning that adds cost without adding value would push organisers away

12What British Cycling should do

The order matters. Each step makes the next one cheaper, and none of them touches the UK Sport money that funds the Olympic programme.

Priority 1 · Strategy

Name gravel as a growth discipline with targets

Add gravel to the strategy to 2029 with numbers attached, such as sanctioned events, gravel members and the share of female and first-time entrants. A discipline that is not in the strategy does not get budget, staff or board attention.

Priority 2 · Organisers

Build the organiser package before the series

Insurance that fits mixed-surface events, a light-touch sanctioning fee, template landowner agreements with Forestry England, Forestry and Land Scotland and private estates, and a shared marketing and entry platform. Work with Cycling UK on rights-of-way access in England and Wales. Organisers should join because it saves them money and effort.

Priority 3 · Competition

A national gravel series with a pathway

Partner existing events rather than replacing them. A five-round series, with The Gralloch as the UCI qualifier and the national championships moving around the regions each year, would give British riders a domestic route to the World Championships.

Priority 4 · Membership

A product for the rider who races but never joined

A gravel membership with event insurance, entry discounts at partner events and series ranking. It is aimed at the 30,000 people a year buying gravel bikes, and at lapsed road members. Membership has fallen for three years in a row. This is the clearest way to reverse it.

Priority 5 · Discovery

Own the searches where riders start

A UK gravel event calendar, a beginner hub, regional route guides and coaching content on britishcycling.org.uk. The domain already ranks and earns AI citations when the content exists. The gap is content, not authority.

Questions British Cycling's own data can answer

  • How many current and lapsed members own a gravel bike or have entered a gravel event?
  • How many entries did the 2026 National Gravel Championships take, and what share were first-time British Cycling event entrants?
  • What does it cost British Cycling to sanction a gravel event, and what would organisers pay for?
  • Where do lapsed road racers go, and how many now ride gravel?
  • What share of the Lloyds partnership's goals could a national gravel series deliver?
